# Conversion psychology for seat-sales pages

Each principle: what it is, why it works on a Cambodian SME owner buying a
business trip, and how it shows up in copy. Use the ones the page has evidence
for. A principle without evidence becomes a cliché and lowers trust.

## Outcome framing (jobs to be done)
The buyer is not hiring a trip, they are hiring a result: lower cost of goods,
a supplier they can trust, a distribution deal, a look at next year's products.
Write the headline and core values as results with a number: "cut 25 to 35%
off broker prices", "walk the factory floor before you wire a dollar".

## Loss aversion
Losing hurts about twice as much as gaining feels good. "Your competitor may be
holding the seat you wanted" and "the same supplier signs one Cambodian
importer" are stronger than "great opportunity". Use it once near the end, not
in every section.

## Anchoring
The first number the reader sees frames every later one. Show the standalone
value of the package (flights, hotel, passes, guide) before the price, then the
price. Show the regular price struck through beside the early-bird price only
while early bird is really open.

## Social proof
People copy people like them. The seat roster with business types, the count of
seats taken, testimonials with a name, role and company. Proof must be real.
A fabricated quote found out costs more than ten real ones earn.

## Scarcity and urgency
Only work when true and specific: 30 seats because the coach has 30 seats,
a deadline because flights must be ticketed. Compute them from data. When a
deadline passes, the page must change by itself to "departs in N days" or
"final seats", never keep the old date.

## Authority and trust transfer
Name the organiser, the expos (Cafe Show Vietnam, Smart City Expo), partner
associations, the coordinator. Borrowed authority is fine if the relationship
is real. Local trust signals matter more than badges: a phone number, Telegram,
ABA bank, a tax invoice.

## Reciprocity and low-commitment first step
Give before asking: the itinerary PDF, a call that answers questions, no payment
today. Make the first step tiny (name and phone) and say what happens next in
the next 15 minutes.

## Risk reversal
State the refund and cancellation terms plainly. "Full refund if the organiser
cancels" and "pay only after the call" remove the fear of being trapped.

## Cognitive ease
Short sentences, one idea each, verbs first on buttons, numbers as digits,
consistent names for the same thing (always "seat", not seat/pass/ticket in
turn). Khmer readers scan slower on phones, so Khmer copy is shorter, not longer.

## Identity and belonging
"Business owners like you", the roster of company types, a named cohort.
The reader should picture themselves in the room and feel they belong there.

## Commitment and consistency
Small choices (pick your industry track, pick your seat) make the reader invest
attention. Carry those choices into the form and the confirmation so the
reservation feels like completing something they already started.

## Peak-end rule
The last thing before the form and the first thing after submitting are what
they remember. End sections on the strongest proof; make the success message
warm, specific and tell them exactly who calls and when.
