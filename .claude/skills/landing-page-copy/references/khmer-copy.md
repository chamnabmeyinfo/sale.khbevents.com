# Khmer copy rules

Khmer readers on this site are business owners reading on a phone. They switch
to Khmer because they want to be sure they understood the money and the
commitment. Khmer copy therefore is shorter, plainer and more concrete than the
English, not a mirror of it.

## Rules
- Write from the intent of the English section, not sentence by sentence.
  Drop metaphors ("cabin", "lock in", "track") and say the plain thing.
- Keep proper nouns in Latin script: Cafe Show Vietnam, Smart City Expo,
  Hanoi, Halong Bay, Telegram, ABA, KHQR, Early Bird. Readers know them that way.
- Prices stay in Latin digits with the dollar sign: $550. Dates and counts may
  use Khmer digits (៨ តុលា ២០២៦, ៣០ កៅអី) but be consistent within a page.
- Address the reader as លោកអ្នក throughout. Never mix with អ្នក in the same
  section.
- Use ។ to end sentences. Avoid English punctuation habits like "—" mid-sentence;
  start a new sentence instead.
- Buttons: verb first. កក់កៅអីឥឡូវ (reserve seat now), ជជែកតាម Telegram.
- Trust words that land: មិនបង់ប្រាក់ថ្ងៃនេះ (no payment today), ទូរស័ព្ទមក
  ក្នុង ១៥ នាទី (calls within 15 minutes), វិក្កយបត្រពន្ធ (tax invoice),
  សងប្រាក់វិញ ១០០% (100% refund).
- Business vocabulary: អ្នកផ្គត់ផ្គង់ (supplier), រោងចក្រ (factory),
  អ្នកនាំចូល (importer), អ្នកចែកចាយ (distributor), តម្លៃបោះដុំ (wholesale price),
  ឈ្មួញកណ្តាល (middleman), សិទ្ធិចែកចាយផ្តាច់មុខ (exclusive distribution rights),
  ការចរចា (negotiation), កូតា MOQ keeps the Latin acronym.
- Where a Khmer word would be long and unfamiliar, keep the English term and
  add a short Khmer gloss once: B2B Matchmaking (ការផ្គូផ្គងដៃគូអាជីវកម្ម).

## QA for Khmer
- Read every string in the mobile layout. Khmer wraps differently; a headline
  that fits in English may need a shorter Khmer form.
- Check numbers and prices match the English exactly.
- Check that every English-only string in the merged content has a Khmer key.
